Privacy Policy

SimNet · Last updated: October 30, 2026

SimNet ("the App") is a Mac menu bar app for iOS developers. It puts iOS simulators into Airplane Mode or onto a slow, lossy network, shows the traffic the apps in them send, and records the simulators' screens. This Privacy Policy explains how the App handles your information. The short version: we don't collect any of it. Everything SimNet sees stays on your Mac, and nothing is ever sent to us.

Information We Do Not Collect

SimNet does not collect, store, transmit, or share any personal data or usage data with us. Specifically, the App:

  • Does not require an account, login, or sign-up with us
  • Does not send any information about you, your Mac, or your apps to us, and we do not operate any servers for the App
  • Does not use analytics, advertising, crash-reporting, or tracking technologies
  • Does not use Location Services, the camera, the microphone, your contacts, or your photos
  • Does not use cookies
  • Does not sell, rent, or share your information with anyone

The Network Extension

SimNet works through a network system extension, SimNet Proxy, that you approve once in System Settings. macOS hands the extension each new connection opened on your Mac so it can check which process opened it. Connections from anything other than an iOS simulator, such as Safari, Mail, or Xcode, are handed straight back to macOS untouched. SimNet doesn't read, record, or change them.

For connections opened by iOS simulators, the extension applies the network condition you chose: it refuses them (Airplane Mode), or relays them on your Mac with limited bandwidth, added delay, and lost data. Relayed connections go to the same servers the simulator's apps were already contacting. SimNet never routes traffic through a server of its own, and it never changes your Mac's own network connection.

SimNet keeps a short list of the apps it has recently affected, by bundle ID and simulator, to show you in its menu. That list stays on your Mac and is cleared when SimNet is switched off or quit.

Network Logging

Network logging is off until you turn it on. While it's on, SimNet records the requests and responses that the apps in the simulators you chose send and receive, including their headers and bodies, so you can view them in its log window. That can include sign-in tokens, cookies, and other personal information the apps send. The log is kept in memory on your Mac, is never sent anywhere, and is deleted when you clear it or quit SimNet. It is only written to disk if you choose Save, to a file and folder you pick. Saved logs contain headers and bodies in full, so treat them as sensitive.

To show HTTPS traffic, SimNet creates its own certificate authority (CA) on your Mac and installs it only into the simulators you choose, using Xcode's own tools. Your Mac itself never trusts it. The CA's private key is stored on your Mac in a folder only you can read, and a copy the extension needs is stored in a folder only the system can read. That copy is deleted when you turn logging off. SimNet checks every server's certificate against your Mac's trust settings and never weakens them.

Screen Recording

SimNet records a simulator's screen only when you choose Record Simulator, using the recorder built into Xcode. It records only the simulator's screen, not the rest of your Mac. To draw your clicks and drags into the video as touches, it follows where you click in that simulator's window while recording. SimNet doesn't need or ask for macOS's Screen Recording or Accessibility permissions.

The recording is held in a temporary folder on your Mac until you save it, to a location you choose, or discard it. Recordings are never uploaded or sent anywhere.

Your Developer Folder

The first time you use Open App Data or record a simulator, SimNet asks you to choose your Developer folder (~/Library/Developer), where Xcode keeps the simulators. SimNet uses it only to list the apps installed on your simulators, open their data folders in Finder when you ask, and hold a screen recording until you save it. It doesn't read or change anything else in that folder.

Information Stored on Your Mac

To keep working between launches, the App saves the following on your Mac:

  • Your settings: the network condition, the simulators and bundle IDs you chose, your custom bandwidth, delay, and loss, and your screen recording preferences
  • Permission to reopen your Developer folder, if you granted it
  • If you've used network logging, SimNet's certificate authority and its private key, and the list of simulators it was installed in

This information stays on your Mac and is never sent to us. Deleting the App removes it. A simulator that SimNet's certificate authority was installed in keeps it until you erase that simulator, or reset its keychain with xcrun simctl keychain <udid> reset. The certificate does nothing while SimNet isn't logging.

Children's Privacy

Because SimNet does not collect any information from any user, it does not knowingly collect information from children under 13 or any other age.

Third-Party Services

SimNet does not integrate with any third-party analytics, advertising, crash-reporting, or other services, and it doesn't contact any server of its own. The only network traffic it handles is the traffic of the apps in your iOS simulators, which it passes on to the servers those apps were already contacting, as described above.
